## Full-recall summary

Johnson produced this material after teaching spectral sequences in a homological algebra course.

His first attempt to explain the filtered-complex construction made the indexing and repeated subquotients hard to see. The response was a large colored-pencil schematic showing the construction spatially, then slides that reveal the diagram in successive layers.

The starting data are a filtered chain complex
\[
\cdots\subset F_{p-1}C_n\subset F_pC_n\subset F_{p+1}C_n\subset\cdots
\]
with differential that does not raise filtration.

Johnson describes an element as “falling \(r\) steps in the filtration” when its differential lands \(r\) filtration levels lower. That language gives the higher differentials a concrete geometric meaning: page \(r\) detects classes whose boundary remains hidden until one looks \(r\) levels away.

For this project this is unusually useful. “How many levels does this object fall?” is much more game-like than “apply the \(r\)-th derived subquotient formula.”

## What it assumes

Johnson explicitly says the notes are **not** a novice introduction to why spectral sequences matter. They assume the reader already knows the basic goal and focus on visualizing the construction.

So the reading order should be:

1. finite maps / fibers;
2. exact sequences;
3. Chow, Hutchings, Vakil or Wandsnider for motivation;
4. Johnson when the player/reader is ready to see why the page-\(r\) differential reaches farther.
