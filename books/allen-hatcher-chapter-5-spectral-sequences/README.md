## Full-recall map

Hatcher describes this as a preliminary, incomplete extra chapter to *Algebraic Topology*, around 110 pages, aimed specifically at spectral sequences in algebraic topology.

### 5.1. Serre spectral sequence

This is the largest part of the chapter.

It proceeds through:

- exact couples;
- the homology Serre spectral sequence;
- Serre classes;
- generalizations and further properties;
- the cohomology Serre spectral sequence;
- rational homotopy groups;
- localization of spaces;
- cohomology of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces;
- computations of homotopy groups of spheres.

The useful structural point for this project is the path

\[
\text{exact couple}
\to
\text{Serre spectral sequence}
\to
\text{geometric information about a fibration}.
\]

A fibration already has the “map plus fibers” language that the pigeonhole game introduces in the finite case. The Serre spectral sequence is therefore not a thematic jump: it is a much richer way to ask how information in the base and fibers constrains the total space.

### 5.2. Adams spectral sequence

Hatcher next develops spectra and the additive Adams spectral sequence, with an application to stable homotopy groups of spheres.

This is later material for the project. It should not drive the first game mechanic, but it is useful evidence that the page/differential language is reusable far beyond one construction.

## Project connection

The most relevant piece is **not** “spectral sequences are advanced.” It is the repeated pattern:

1. start with a map;
2. identify pieces/fibers/quotients;
3. arrange exact information;
4. derive successive approximations;
5. use what cannot move or must cancel to infer the result.

That is close to a game loop.
